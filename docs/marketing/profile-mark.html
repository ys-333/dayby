Before you pick

Two constraints decide this, and neither is aesthetic.

Instagram shows your avatar at about 40px in the feed and in comments — roughly a fingernail. The 176px version below is decoration; the 40px row is the actual test. Anything with fine detail, an outline-only form, or more than one idea in it dissolves there.

Your app icon is still the default Flutter logo. So this isn't only a profile picture — it's the launcher icon, the Play Store icon, and the mark on every graphic you post. Pick once, use everywhere. That also means it must survive Android's adaptive-icon circle mask, which crops harder than Instagram does.

What to avoid

The habit-app category is saturated with four marks: a checkmark, a flame, a leaf/sprout, and a calendar square. All four are taken, none is ownable, and a flame in particular signals exactly the streak culture you're arguing against. None of the candidates below uses one.

The candidates

Same geometry rendered at four sizes. The 24px column is the comment-thread size — a mark that survives there survives anywhere.

What it is

The ◐ glyph from your own status model — the symbol your app already draws for a partially-completed commitment, at full size.

Why it wins

It's the only candidate that states the thesis. Every other habit app's icon means "done"; yours means "half counts". That's the whole argument compressed into one shape, and it gives you a line for the launch post: the icon is a half-finished day, on purpose. It holds at 24px, it's a single unbroken form under the adaptive-icon mask, and it's already in your product's visual vocabulary.

The honest objection

A half-filled circle is also a generic progress ring and a moon phase. It's distinctive in this category, not in the abstract. The tight crop and heavy stroke below are what pull it away from a stock progress indicator.

What it is

Nine days. Seven done, one skipped (the dash), one not yet (the outline). A week that isn't perfect and doesn't need to be.

The case for it

Conceptually the richest of the four and the most ownable — nobody else would ship an icon with a hole in it. It's also a direct crop of your History screen, so icon and product look like the same thing.

Why it doesn't win

Look at the 24px column. The dash and the outline cell — the two elements carrying the entire meaning — are the first things to vanish, and what's left is a generic grid that reads as a menu button. A mark whose point disappears at the size it's mostly seen at has the point backwards. Excellent as a highlight cover and as a post graphic; wrong as an avatar.

What it is

A broken ring with a dot re-entering the gap. The streak breaks; you come back. Recovery time, drawn.

The case for it

The most sophisticated idea here and a perfect match for your headline metric. It looks like a real company's mark rather than an app sticker.

Why it doesn't win

Nobody decodes it unaided. The gap reads as a design flourish, not a broken streak, and the dot reads as a decorative accent — you'd be explaining your logo forever. Symbols that need a caption are a luxury for brands people already recognise. Worth revisiting at 10,000 followers.

What it is

A lowercase d for Dayby whose bowl is the ◐ partial glyph. Letterform and symbol in one.

The case for it

Letterforms build name recall faster than abstract symbols, which matters at zero brand recognition. This one smuggles the thesis into the letter rather than sitting beside it.

Why it doesn't win

Two problems. The stem forces the bowl off-centre, which fights the circle crop — under Android's adaptive mask the stem crowds the edge. And the app is Riyaz by Dayby; anchoring the permanent mark to the letter d bets the identity on the half of the name you've said is the container, not the product. Keep it as a wordmark lockup, not the avatar.

One decision for you

Generating the Android icon set from a single source normally uses flutter_launcher_icons — a dev dependency. Your CLAUDE.md says not to add a package without asking, so: yes or no? If no, the five mipmap PNGs can be exported by hand instead; it's slower but adds nothing to pubspec.yaml.

Where the other three still earn their keep

  • B — Honest grid: the Why highlight cover, and the recurring motif on carousel slide 1. It works everywhere it's bigger than a thumbnail.
  • C — Return: the Build highlight cover, and the graphic for any post about recovery time.
  • D — d-bowl: the wordmark lockup for the landing page footer and the Play Store feature graphic.

Four marks from one system — same green, same 26-unit stroke, same figure colour — which is what makes a young account look established.
